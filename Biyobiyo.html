Porcine Models in Heparin Production and Their Biomedical Significance

Publication Date: 2026

Heparin is one of the most critical biochemical compounds in medicine that prevents blood clotting (anticoagulant). It saves millions of patients' lives every day, from open-heart surgeries to kidney dialysis and vascular obstruction treatments.

The Critical Role of Porcine Tissues in Medicine

A large portion of the global heparin supply is derived from porcine small intestine mucosa. The high similarity of porcine tissues to human physiology makes them the most reliable biological source in both heparin extraction and xenotransplantation (organ transplantation) research.

In this article, the molecular structure of heparin, porcine-derived heparin extraction processes, the current state of synthetic alternatives, and the bioethical dimensions of this biomedical use are examined with scientific data.
